1. And the second commandment of the Teaching; 2. Thou shalt not commit murder, thou shalt not commit adultery,<ref>

Ex. xx. 13, 14.  &#160;</ref> thou shalt not commit p&#230;derasty,<ref>

Or, &#8220;corrupt boys,&#8221; as in the version of {{anchor+|viii.iii.ii-p4.1|2=Apostolic Constitutions}}.  &#160;</ref> thou shalt not commit fornication, thou shalt not steal,<ref>

Ex. xx. 15.  &#160;</ref> thou shalt not practice magic, thou shalt not practice witchcraft, thou shalt not murder a child by abortion nor kill that which is begotten.<ref>

Comp. Ex. xxi. 22, 23. The Codex reads &#947;&#949;&#957;&#957;&#951;&#952;&#8051;&#957;&#964;&#945;, which Schaff renders &#8220;the new-born child.&#8221; Bryennios substitutes &#947;&#949;&#957;&#957;&#951;&#952;&#8051;&#957;, which is accepted by most editors, and rendered as above. &#160;</ref> Thou shalt not covet the things of thy neighbour,<ref>

Ex. xx. 17.  &#160;</ref> 3. thou shalt not forswear thyself,<ref>

Matt. v. 34.  &#160;</ref> thou shalt not bear false witness,<ref>

Ex. xx. 16.  &#160;</ref> thou shalt not speak evil, thou shalt bear no grudge.<ref>

Rendered &#8220;nor shalt thou be mindful of injuries&#8221; in version of {{anchor+|viii.iii.ii-p10.1|2=Apostolic Constitutions}}.  &#160;</ref> 4. Thou shalt not be double-minded nor double-tongued; for to be double-tongued is a  snare of death.<ref>

So Barnabas, xix.  &#160;</ref> 5. Thy speech shall not be false, nor empty, but fulfilled by deed.<ref>

Verse 5, except the first clause, occurs only here.  &#160;</ref> 6. Thou shalt not be covetous, nor rapacious, nor a hypocrite, nor evil disposed, nor haughty. Thou shalt not take evil counsel against thy neighbour.<ref>

Latter half of verse 6 in Barnabas, xix.  &#160;</ref> 7. Thou shalt not hate any man; but some thou shalt reprove,<ref>

Lev. xix. 17; {{anchor+|viii.iii.ii-p14.2|2=Apostolic Constitutions}}.  &#160;</ref> and concerning some thou shalt pray, and some thou shalt love more than thy own life.<ref>

Or, &#8220;soul.&#8221; The last part of the clause is found in Barnabas; but &#8220;and concerning some&#8230;pray, and some&#8221; has no parallel. An interesting verse in its literary history. &#160;</ref>  &#160;
