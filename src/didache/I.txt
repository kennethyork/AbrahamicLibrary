'''{{bbsc|Chapter I.&#8212;The Two Ways; The First Commandment.}}'''

1. {{small-caps|There}} are two ways,<ref>

This phrase connects the book with the {{anchor+|viii.iii.i-p2.1|2=Du&#230; Vi&#230;}}; see Introductory Notice. Barnabas has &#8220;light&#8221; and &#8220;darkness&#8221; for &#8220;life&#8221; and &#8220;death.&#8221; &#160;</ref> one of life and one of death;<ref>

Deut. xxx. 15, 19; Jer. xxi. 8; Matt. vii. 13, 14  &#160;</ref> but a great difference between the two ways. 2. The way of life, then, is this: First, thou shalt love God<ref>

Comp. Deut. vi. 5, which is fully cited in {{anchor+|viii.iii.i-p4.4|2=Apostolic Constitutions}}, vii. 2, though the verb here is more exactly cited from LXX. &#160;</ref> who made thee; second, thy neighbour as thyself;<ref>

Lev. xix. 18; Matt. xxii. 37, 39. Comp. Mark xii. 30, 31  &#160;</ref> and all things whatsoever thou wouldst should not occur to thee, thou also to another do not do.<ref>

Comp. Tobit iv. 15; and Matt. vii. 12; Luke vi. 31  &#160;</ref> 3. And of these sayings<ref>

These Old-Testament commands are thus taught by the Lord.  &#160;</ref> the teaching is this: Bless them that curse you, and pray for your enemies, and fast for them that persecute you.<ref>

Matt. v. 44. But the last clause is added, and is of unknown origin; not found in {{anchor+|viii.iii.i-p8.3|2=Apostolic Constitutions}}  &#160;</ref> For what thank is there, if ye love them that love you? Do not also the Gentiles do the same?<ref>

Matt. v. 46, 47; Luke vi. 32. The two passages are combined. &#160;</ref> But do ye love them that hate you; and ye shall not have an enemy.<ref>

So Apostolic Constitutions. Comp. 1 Pet. iii. 13  &#160;</ref> 4. Abstain thou from fleshly and worldly lusts.<ref>

1 Pet. ii. 11. The Codex has &#963;&#969;&#956;&#945;&#964;&#953;&#954;&#8182;&#957;, &#8220;bodily;&#8221; but editors correct to &#954;&#959;&#963;&#956;&#953;&#954;&#8182;&#957;  &#160;</ref> If one give thee a blow upon thy right cheek, turn to him the other also;<ref>

Matt. v. 39; Luke vi. 29. &#160;</ref> and thou shalt be perfect. If one impress thee for one mile, go with him two.<ref>

Matt. v. 41  &#160;</ref> If one take away thy cloak, give him also thy coat.<ref>

Matt. v. 40; Luke vi. 29  &#160;</ref> If one take from thee thine own, ask it not back,<ref>

Luke vi. 30. The last clause is a peculiar addition: &#8220;art not able,&#8221; since thou art a Christian; otherwise it is a commonplace observation. &#160;</ref> for indeed thou art not able. 5. Give to every one that asketh thee, and ask it not back;<ref>

Luke vi. 30. The rest of the sentence is explained by the parallel passage in {{anchor+|viii.iii.i-p16.2|2=Apostolic Constitutions}}, which cites Matt. v. 45.  &#160;</ref> for the Father willeth that to all should be given of our own blessings (free gifts).<ref>

Bryennios finds a parallel (or citation) in {{anchor+|viii.iii.i-p17.2|2=Hermas}}, Commandment Second, p. 20, vol. i. {{anchor+|viii.iii.i-p17.4|2=Ante-Nicene Fathers}}. The remainder of this chapter has no parallel in {{anchor+|viii.iii.i-p17.5|2=Apostolic Constitutions}}. &#160;</ref> Happy is he that giveth according to the commandment; for he is guiltless. Woe to him that receiveth; for if one having need receiveth, he is guiltless; but he that receiveth not having need, shall pay the penalty, why he received and for what, and, coming into straits (confinement),<ref>

Gr. &#7952;&#957; &#963;&#965;&#957;&#959;&#967;&#8135;. Probably = imprisonment; see next clause.  &#160;</ref> he shall be examined concerning the things which he hath done, and he shall not escape thence until he pay back the last farthing.<ref>
