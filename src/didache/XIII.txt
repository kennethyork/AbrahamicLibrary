'''{{bbsc|Chapter XIII.<ref>

A large part of this chapter is found in {{anchor+|viii.iii.xiii-p1.1|2=Apostolic Constitutions,}} vii. 28, 29, but with modifications and additions indicating a later date. &#160;</ref>&#8212;Support of Prophets.}}'''

1. But every true prophet that willeth to abide among you<ref>

&#8220;Who will settle among you&#8221; (Hitchcock and Brown). The itinerant prophets might become stationary, we infer. Chaps. xi.-xv. point to a movement from an itinerant and extraordinary ministry to a more settled one. &#160;</ref> is worthy of his support.<ref>

Lit., &#8220;nourishment,&#8221; &#8220;food.&#8221;  &#160;</ref> 2. So also a true teacher is himself worthy, as the workman, of his support.<ref>

Matt. x. 10; comp. Luke x. 7.  &#160;</ref> 3. Every first-fruit, therefore, of the products of wine-press and threshing-floor, of oxen and of sheep, thou shalt take and give to the prophets, for they are your high priests.<ref>

This phrase, indicating a sacerdotal view of the ministry, seems to point to a later date than that claimed for the {{anchor+|viii.iii.xiii-p6.1|2=Teaching}}. Some regard it as an interpolation: others take it in a figurative sense. In {{anchor+|viii.iii.xiii-p6.2|2=Apostolic Constitutions}} the sacerdotal view is more marked. [1 Pet. ii. 9. If the plebs = &#8220;priests,&#8221; prophets = &#8220;high priests.&#8221;] Here the term is restricted to the prophets: compare Schaff in loco. &#160;</ref> 4. But if ye have not a prophet, give it to the poor. 5. If thou makest a batch of dough, take the first-fruit and give according to the commandment. 6. So also when thou openest a jar of wine or of oil, take the first-fruit and give it to the prophets; 7. and of money (silver) and clothing and every possession, take the first-fruit, as it may seem good to thee, and give according to the commandment. &#160;
