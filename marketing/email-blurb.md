## Short blurb (for a link in an existing newsletter)

**Apocrypha Plus** gathers the seventy-seven writings the early church read,
copied and argued over — and then set aside: the deuterocanon, the wider
apocrypha, Enoch entire, the pseudepigrapha, the New Testament apocrypha, and
the Apostolic Fathers. Five public-domain sources spanning three centuries are
brought to one modern American English, with 805 corrections each checked in
place and the translators' word order left untouched. Twenty-two of the harder
books open with a page of plain-language orientation, and a summary of all
sixty-six books of the common canon closes the volume. 786 pages, two columns,
footnotes at the foot of the column that holds them, set in Gentium Plus.
Everything is public domain — and so is this edition.

## Longer blurb (standalone announcement)

**Apocrypha Plus: the books outside the sixty-six, in one volume.**

Most collections give you one slice of this material. A volume of apocrypha
stops at fourteen titles. A volume of the Apostolic Fathers stops at the
Fathers. Apocrypha Plus puts the whole shelf between two covers.

Inside:

- **The Deuterocanonical Books** — Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach, Baruch,
  1 and 2 Maccabees, and the additions to Esther and Daniel.
- **The Wider Apocrypha** — 1 and 2 Esdras, the Prayer of Manasseh, Psalm 151,
  3 and 4 Maccabees.
- **The Book of Enoch**, entire.
- **The Pseudepigrapha** — Adam and Eve, the Secrets of Enoch, the Psalms and
  Odes of Solomon, Aristeas, Ahikar, the Testaments of the Twelve Patriarchs.
- **The New Testament Apocrypha** — the Protevangelion, the infancy gospels,
  Nicodemus, Laodiceans, Paul and Thecla, Paul and Seneca.
- **The Apostolic Fathers** — the Didache, Clement, Barnabas, Ignatius,
  Polycarp, Diognetus, the Shepherd of Hermas, Papias.

One English, not five. Five public-domain sources spanning three centuries are
brought to a single modern American standard — no crossing from 1926 prose
into 1870 prose mid-volume. 805 corrections, each checked in place. The
translators' own word order is untouched.

Written to be entered, not just owned. Twenty-two of the harder books open
with a page of plain-language orientation. A summary of each of the sixty-six
books of the common canon closes the volume, cross-referenced to the works
printed here.

Made to be read. Two columns, footnotes at the foot of the column that holds
them, a binding margin wide enough for a book this thick. 786 pages, set in
Gentium Plus.

Every text in this book is in the public domain — and so is this edition of
it.
