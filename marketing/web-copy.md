# Apocrypha Plus — web page copy

## Hero

**Apocrypha Plus**

The books the early church read, copied and argued over — and then set aside.

Seventy-seven writings outside the sixty-six, gathered in one volume.

## Intro

Most collections give you one slice of this material. A volume of apocrypha
stops at fourteen titles. A volume of the Apostolic Fathers stops at the
Fathers. Apocrypha Plus puts the whole shelf between two covers.

## What's inside

- **The Deuterocanonical Books** — Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach, Baruch,
  1 and 2 Maccabees, with the Rest of Esther, the Epistle of Jeremiah,
  Susanna, Bel and the Dragon, and the Song of the Three.
- **The Wider Apocrypha** — 1 and 2 Esdras, the Prayer of Manasseh, Psalm 151,
  3 and 4 Maccabees.
- **The Book of Enoch**, entire.
- **The Pseudepigrapha** — the two books of Adam and Eve, the Secrets of
  Enoch, the Psalms and Odes of Solomon, the Letter of Aristeas, the Story of
  Ahikar, and the Testaments of the Twelve Patriarchs.
- **The New Testament Apocrypha** — the Protevangelion, the two infancy
  gospels, Nicodemus, the letter to the Laodiceans, Paul and Thecla, and the
  correspondence of Paul and Seneca.
- **The Apostolic Fathers** — the Didache, Clement, Barnabas, the seven
  letters of Ignatius, Polycarp and the account of his death, Diognetus, the
  Shepherd of Hermas, and the fragments of Papias.

## One English, not five

Five public-domain sources spanning three centuries have been brought to a
single modern American standard. Spelling, pronouns, verb forms and the older
constructions are all present-day — no crossing from 1926 prose into 1870
prose in the middle of a volume. Eight hundred and five corrections, every one
checked in place before the rule was written. The translators' own word order
is left untouched.

## Written to be entered, not just owned

Twenty-two of the harder books open with a page of plain-language orientation:
who these people were, what happens, and what to expect from the way it is
written. Ignatius wrote his letters chained to ten guards on the road to his
own execution — that is worth knowing before the first sentence.

A summary of each of the sixty-six books of the common canon closes the
volume, cross-referenced to the works printed here. You can see where Baruch
attaches to Jeremiah, why 1 Esdras retells Ezra, and why Jude quotes Enoch as
scripture.
